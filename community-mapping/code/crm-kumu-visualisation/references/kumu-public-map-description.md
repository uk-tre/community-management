## Project description (summary level info)

### Why are we building a community map?

We are working to identify and connect the people, groups and organisations who make up the UK Trusted Research Environment (TRE) community.
We are committed to supporting the growth, impact, transparency, diversity and collaboration of this community, and to developing and sharing good practice for safe, equitable use of sensitive data in research.

To achieve this, we need to understand the community's priorities, what its members care about, and what shared infrastructure and support would help them succeed.
Importantly, we also need to identify the people and groups who can contribute to setting the community's strategic direction.

**To do this, we need to know who the community are, so that we can develop appropriate ways to listen, communicate and work together.**

### Why is this work important to us?

UK TRE is a community of people who build, run, govern, use and are affected by trusted research environments.
Many of us work in separate organisations and working groups, and it is hard to see who else is doing similar work, or who could help.
A well-managed, transparent community map helps us connect people, avoid duplicated effort, and make sure that no organisation, role or group is left out of decisions that affect them.

The map is built and managed by [RCM Cooperative](https://www.rcmcooperative.com) on behalf of the UK TRE Community, with Manchester University NHS Foundation Trust (MFT) as data controller.
We are documenting the work as we go, so that other communities can learn from it and reuse the materials.
See the [RCM-CRM repository on GitHub](https://github.com/rcmcooperative/rcm-crm/).

## Presentation slides

### Slide 1: Why a community map?

Trusted research environments let approved researchers use sensitive data, such as health records, safely and for public benefit.
The UK has many TREs, run by many organisations, and the people who work in and around them are spread across institutions, sectors and disciplines.

When we cannot see each other, we duplicate work, miss opportunities to collaborate, and risk leaving out the people who are least visible, including patients, the public and smaller organisations.

**We believe that knowing who is in our community is the first step to building it together.**

This map shows the people, organisations and working groups of the UK TRE community that we know about, and how they are connected.

### Slide 2: Research community management

We want to know who is part of this community, what they are working on, and how we can support them.

**Through professional research community management, we are creating opportunities for connection and leadership across the community.
We want to design these opportunities around your needs, and we can only do that if we know who you are.**

Learn more about [RCM Cooperative](https://www.rcmcooperative.com) and our approach to community management.

### Slide 3: Roadmap

#### Now

We are in the *discovery* phase: finding the people and groups in the community, and listening to what they need.

**We want to find more people and organisations working with TREs and engaging with UK TRE.**

You may already be on our map.
If so, you will receive an email inviting you to check the data we hold about you and to choose how you appear.

**If you would like to be on the map, you can [add yourself here](http://tinyurl.com/uktre-join) by signing up to the UK TRE Slack or Jisc mailing list.**

#### Next

**Strategy.**
We will use what we learn from the map, and from listening to community members, to develop the community's mission and strategic priorities together.

**Reporting.**
We will analyse the community's make-up, including who is represented, which organisations and roles are well or thinly represented, and how working groups are connected, and report back on how it changes over time.
We will also publish summaries.

**Publication of materials.**
We will continue to develop and openly publish the documentation and tools used here, and invite other communities to test and reuse them.
See the [RCM-CRM repository](https://github.com/rcmcooperative/rcm-crm/).

**Continued discovery.**
Finding and connecting community members is ongoing.
The map will be updated as people validate their details and as new people join.

### Slide 4: Explore the map

We are using this map to plan how we support the community, **and** we want you to use it to find colleagues, future collaborators and working groups.

This is a public version of the map, so what you can see depends on the choices people have made.
People who have chosen to be **identifiable** appear with their name.
People who have chosen to be **pseudonymised** appear with a key (for example *Person-1a2b3c4d*) instead of a name.
We show nobody who has not given us permission to do so.

Organisations appear on the public map only when someone affiliated with them has validated their details and agreed to be shown publicly.
All working and interest groups are shown.
So the map shows part of the community, not all of it, and a missing organisation does not mean it is not part of UK TRE.
**We hope you will check your details and choose to be visible, so that we all benefit from seeing who is working in this space.**

You will notice clusters, where many people are connected to one organisation or group.
We will look at how to make the most of these clusters, and how to connect people who are less visible.

### Slide 5: How to navigate

Click the three grey dots next to this panel to close the text and see more of the map.

#### Clustering

Elements that are connected are drawn closer together.
Connections come from affiliations (a person works at an organisation) and from working or interest groups (a person is a member or lead of a group).
Connections are our routes for communication and sharing.

#### Zoom

Zoom in and out using the "+" and "-".

#### Filter

Use the dropdowns at the top left to filter the map by:

- **type**: for example people only, working groups only, or any combination of organisation types.
- **career stage**: where people have chosen to share it.
- **career role type**: where people have chosen to share it.
- **connection type**: lead, member or affiliation.

**Example filtering.**
Try filtering with the controls at the top left.
Use the "FILTER BY TYPE" control to show only people.
See how the distance between people changes when you add organisations, and again when you add working groups.
Notice what happens to the relationships when you remove organisational boundaries.

1. type = people
2. type = people + working groups
3. type = people + working groups + academic institutes
4. type = people + public sector / government

#### Showcase

"Showcase" keeps every element on screen but highlights the ones you select, by type, career stage or career role type.

#### Search

Use the search bar to find a person (if they have chosen to be identifiable) or the name of an organisation or working group.
The element will be brought to the centre of your screen.

#### Metadata

Click on any element, then on the three dots, to see the information we hold about it.
We show only what a person has agreed to share.

#### Connections

Hover over an element to highlight the elements it is connected to.

There are three types of connection:

- **Affiliation**: a person is affiliated with an organisation, for example where they work.
- **Member**: a person is a member of a working or interest group.
- **Lead**: a person leads a working or interest group.

Use the "FILTER BY CONNECTION TYPE" control at the top left to show only the types you choose.
Show only affiliations to see how people cluster around organisations.
Show only member and lead connections to see how working and interest groups are linked through the people who belong to several of them.
Show all three to see how the two views fit together.

### Slide 6: Adding your data

Join the map by signing up to our Slack and/or Jisc workspace here: [add yourself here](http://tinyurl.com/uktre-join)

We will periodically pull data from these sources and send new members an individual link to validate the information we hold about them, and add more metadata as required.

When you validate your details, you choose how you appear on the map:

- **Public, identifiable**: your name, organisation and any details you share are shown on the public map.
- **Public, pseudonymised**: you appear as a key (for example *Person-1a2b3c4d*) instead of a name. Your organisation and connections are shown.
- **Internal only**: you are on our internal map, used for planning, but not on the public map.
- **None**: you are not shown on any map.

Read how we use and protect your data: [Participant Information Sheet](https://tinyurl.com/uktre-map-PIS)

Data are processed in line with UK GDPR, with Manchester University NHS Foundation Trust as data controller (REC 23/NE/0076, DPIA B01917, ISO 27001 IS806270).

Review our documentation and materials on the template structure which has been used to create this map: [RCM-CRM on GitHub](https://github.com/rcmcooperative/rcm-crm/)
